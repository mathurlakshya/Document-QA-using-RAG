The Water Cycle

The water cycle describes how water moves continuously between Earth's surface and the atmosphere.

Evaporation happens when energy from the Sun heats water in oceans, lakes, rivers, and other surface waters. Liquid water changes into water vapour and rises into the atmosphere. Plants also release water vapour through a process called transpiration.

As water vapour rises and cools, condensation occurs. The vapour changes into tiny liquid water droplets or ice crystals, forming clouds.

When droplets or ice crystals become heavy enough, water falls back to Earth's surface as precipitation. Precipitation can take the form of rain, snow, sleet, or hail.

Some precipitation flows over the land into streams, rivers, lakes, and oceans. This is called runoff. Some water also soaks into the ground and becomes groundwater.

The Sun provides much of the energy that drives the water cycle. The cycle has no single starting or ending point; its stages happen continuously and help distribute fresh water around the planet.
